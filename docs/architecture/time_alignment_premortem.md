# Pre-mortem: Agent9 running its own process to time-align KPIs

**Status:** pre-mortem, 2026-10-18, requested by the product owner. No decision taken; this records
the risks so they are on hand when the trend and data-contract questions are decided.
**Related:** `DEVELOPMENT_BACKLOG.md` Phase 25 (Canonical Temporal Alignment) and Phase 26 (Named
Period Comparisons); `data_product_time_dimension_planning.md`; `period_comparisons_decisions.md`.

## The approach examined

When a client's data products do not carry a usable time dimension, Agent9 builds and maintains its
own: it infers time keys, rewrites each KPI's query to slice it by period, maps fiscal calendars,
picks which business event dates a KPI, and works out where actuals end. That is a second process
for time, parallel to the client's own reporting.

The question arose on 2026-10-18 when two lubricants Sales KPIs (Gross Revenue, Sales Tax Collected)
could be read but had no trend: the monthly series is built by rewriting a KPI's query, and the
rewrite does not understand their nested query.

## Late 2027: the approach failed. The likely reasons

**1. The query rewriting broke on real clients' SQL, sometimes silently.** Agent9 slices a KPI by
month by pattern-matching its query and rewriting it. Real KPIs, written by client analysts or
generated by their semantic layer, use joins, CTEs, window functions and dialect quirks. Each new
shape fails: usually visibly ("trend unavailable"), occasionally by running and returning a plausible
wrong number.
*Signs already present:* the nested-query failure above; period labels "2026-00" and "2026-012"
from mishandled fiscal periods; the hardcoded `transaction_date` guess removed in Phase 10F (May
2026) and reintroduced three months later in a new copy (Phase 25 root cause); three hand-written
copies of the same rewrite in Situation Awareness alone.

**2. Agent9 became a second source of truth for time, and lost the argument.** The CFO's management
pack says Q3 revenue was X; Agent9's own alignment says Y, because of period-end adjustments,
recognition against posting dates, restatements, or a 4-4-5 retail calendar. The first meeting where
a card disagrees with the pack is the last.
*Signs:* Phase 25 found 90% of sales order lines fall in a different month from their revenue
recognition, with period figures off by 5.5–8.9%. The demo data is generated by Agent9's own code, so
it agrees with Agent9's assumptions by construction and cannot reveal this class of problem.

**3. It grew into a semantic-layer product.** Each client brings a calendar variation: fiscal year
start, 53-week years, weekly operational data, late or reopened periods, plan versions. The alignment
layer absorbed the engineering time and the decision pipeline (Situation Awareness → Deep Analysis →
Solution Finder → Value Assurance), which is the product's distinctive value, stalled.
*Signs:* the stated product direction is that the Data Product Agent stays a thin adapter over the
client's semantic layer, and that Agent9 does not build natural-language-to-SQL. This approach is the
same thing for time.

**4. It could not follow the connectivity strategy.** The plan is MCP-first: ask the client's governed
semantic layer for "metric by month". A process built on rewriting raw SQL has nothing to rewrite
there, so it either blocks MCP adoption or becomes a dead end when MCP goes live. Rewriting queries
against base tables can also bypass the client's governed views and row-level rules.

**5. No one could explain a number.** "Where does this figure come from?" got the answer "we
inferred the date column, rewrote your query, and cut it off at the last month our series showed."
That fails the owner's explainability test for the product, and would fail an auditor's.

**6. Failures looked like quiet.** "Show nothing rather than something wrong" is the right rule, but
as inference rules multiplied, absences became normal and nobody noticed which KPIs were not being
judged.
*Signs:* five lubricants Sales KPIs are never scanned (Situation Awareness loads only "Finance"
domain KPIs) and two others were unreadable for weeks; both were found by accident on 2026-10-17/18.

**7. Cost and speed degraded at enterprise scale.** Alignment by re-querying (series first, per-month
reads) multiplies warehouse queries.
*Sign:* a 24-KPI offline assessment ran past ten minutes on 2026-10-16.

## What survives: declared and thin, not inferred and rewritten

- **Keep: translation of what the source declares.** TimeFilter turning a declared fiscal year and
  period, or a declared date column, into a filter; Phase 25's canonical period label (YYYY-MM).
  Phase 26 build choice 4 (cards read each KPI on its declared time dimension) belongs here: it uses
  declarations, not guesses.
- **Make it a contract.** At onboarding a data product declares its time dimensions, and each KPI is
  written in a shape that can be grouped by period. Alternatively the source provides a monthly view,
  or, with MCP, the semantic layer answers "by month" itself.
- **Refuse instead of inventing.** A KPI that does not meet the contract is listed as "not judged: no
  usable time dimension", rather than reconstructed inside Agent9.

## Bearing on open items (2026-10-18)

- **Trends for nested-query KPIs** (open). Extending the query rewrite to nested queries is failure 1;
  reading each month separately is failure 7. The contract path suggests fixing the data product
  instead: for example, the Sales data product exposing one row per order with its fiscal period, so
  Gross Revenue becomes a simple total and gets a trend from the existing builder. Claude had first
  recommended extending the rewrite; this pre-mortem argues against that recommendation.
- **Phase 26 decision 7** (actuals cut-off worked out from the data; ruled 2026-10-17) belongs to the
  same inference family. A declared "actuals closed through" date from the source would be the
  contract version. Flagged here, not reopened.
- **Phase 26 build choice 4** (reading on the declared date) fits the declared-and-thin side.
